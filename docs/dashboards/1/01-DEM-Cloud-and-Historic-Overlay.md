# Dashboard 1 — DEM Cloud & Historic Overlays

## What this chart shows

When an earthquake breaks the ground surface, the fault doesn't tear along a
single clean line — it churns up a band of broken, warped soil and leaves a step
behind. This chart plots the two measurements that describe that damage: the
width of the disturbed zone (DZW) on the horizontal axis, and the height of the
resulting step (scarp height) on the vertical.

Two very different sources share these axes. The dense cloud of faint points is a
computer simulation that models the ground as millions of interacting grains and
runs thousands of earthquakes under varying conditions. The bold markers are
real, well-documented earthquakes — Chi-Chi, Wenchuan, Kashmir, and the 1952 Kern
County event — of which only a handful exist, because surface ruptures measured
this thoroughly are rare. The chart answers one question: does the simulation
behave like the real world? When the real earthquakes fall within the simulated
cloud, that is evidence the model can be trusted to estimate how severely the
ground might rupture near a fault — precisely where real measurements are almost
always too sparse to rely on alone.
